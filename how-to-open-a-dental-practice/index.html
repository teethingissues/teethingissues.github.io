How to open a dental practice in the UK: CQC right first time, and a full diary sooner.

For dentists and dental entrepreneurs opening a new or squat practice. Written by dental care professionals, including a dental care professional, CQC expert and former CQC Registered Manager, who is also a growth business consultant for dental practices.

How do you open a dental practice in the UK?

Opening a dental practice in the UK means doing nine things in roughly this order. Most delays come from starting the later steps too late, so plan backwards from your opening date:

  1. Decide who the practice is for: the patients you want, the treatments you’ll offer, and private, NHS or mixed.
  2. Build the numbers: set-up costs, monthly running costs and how long your money lasts before the diary fills.
  3. Find premises and check planning use and the lease.
  4. Design and fit out the surgeries and decontamination room.
  5. Register with the Care Quality Commission (CQC) in England, or the regulator for your nation.
  6. Put compliance in place: staff checks, radiation, decontamination, emergencies and risk assessments.
  7. Hire and onboard your first team, with complete staff files.
  8. Set up phones, booking and follow-up so every enquiry becomes an appointment.
  9. Tell people before you open, and track calls, bookings and new patients from week 1.

How much does it cost to open a dental practice in the UK?

Published UK estimates for a new squat practice range widely. Practice Plan puts a typical private squat at around £250,000 to £300,000 (2023), Livingstones Accountants suggests £100,000 to £300,000 or more for premises, equipment and set-up (2025), and myPOS estimates £250,000 to £500,000, with equipment alone often over £100,000 (2026). The biggest costs are usually the fit-out, dental chairs and equipment, X-ray, decontamination and IT. On top of set-up, budget for several months of running costs (rent, wages, software, insurance and marketing) while the diary fills. For example, a practice that opens with an empty diary still pays its team from day 1, which is why a pre-opening patient plan matters as much as the fit-out.

Do I need to register a new dental practice with CQC?

Yes, in England. Any provider of dental treatment in England must be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC) before treating patients. Registration looks at the provider, the premises and how the practice will be run, and in most cases a registered manager is named too. The other UK nations have their own regulators: Healthcare Inspectorate Wales (HIW) in Wales, Healthcare Improvement Scotland for independent clinics in Scotland, and the Regulation and Quality Improvement Authority (RQIA) in Northern Ireland. Start the application early, because the opening date can’t move forward until registration is complete.

What does CQC look at when a new dental practice registers?

CQC judges dental practices against five key questions: is the service safe, effective, caring, responsive and well-led? For a new practice, that means showing how each will be true from the first patient. In practice, CQC wants to see that the premises are suitable, that staff will be properly checked and trained, that decontamination and infection control follow national guidance, that medical emergencies are planned for, and that the person in charge understands their responsibilities. For example, a registered manager who can explain how staff files, emergency drugs and decontamination logs will be checked each week gives a much stronger account than a folder of unread templates.

What compliance do I need in place before opening?

  • Staff checks: Enhanced DBS checks, GDC registration and indemnity for clinicians, and Hepatitis B immunity records with blood test results.
  • Radiation: registration with the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) under the Ionising Radiations Regulations 2017 (IRR17), a Radiation Protection Adviser and local rules.
  • Decontamination: a set-up that follows HTM 01-05 in England, with daily, weekly and quarterly autoclave tests logged.
  • Medical emergencies: the full kit and drugs, weekly checks and trained staff.
  • Risk assessments: fire, legionella and COSHH.
  • Data: registration with the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO).
  • Clinical waste: a licensed collection contract.

Each item needs an owner and a review date from day 1, so nothing quietly goes out of date in year one.

How do I hire the first team for a new dental practice?

Hire earlier than feels comfortable. A new practice usually needs dental nurses, a receptionist or treatment coordinator, and a practice manager role covered, even if the owner covers it at first. Every new starter needs a complete staff file before they see a patient: Enhanced DBS, GDC registration where required, Hepatitis B records, references, contract, induction and training records. For example, a nurse who starts with no Hepatitis B blood test result on file is a gap an inspector will find, so check each file in full before the first shift, not after.

How does a new dental practice get its first patients?

  • Start before you open. Share who the practice is for and what makes it different, locally and on social media, months before day 1.
  • Set up your Google Business Profile early, and ask every early patient for a review. Google’s rules say a business must not offer anything in return for a review.
  • Answer every call from opening day, including lunch and after five. In the first months, a missed call is often a lost first patient.
  • Follow up every enquiry and every treatment plan that isn’t booked.
  • Track calls, bookings and new patients from week 1, so you can see what’s working while there’s still time to change it.

What are the most common mistakes when opening a squat practice?

  • Planning without acting: the opening date keeps moving while costs run.
  • Templates never finished: compliance documents downloaded but never made to fit the practice.
  • Hiring too late: the team isn’t trained or fully checked when patients arrive.
  • Phones as an afterthought: calls ring out in the first weeks, when every patient matters most.
  • Waiting for patients to find you: no pre-opening plan, so the diary fills slowly while the rent goes out.

Should I get help opening a dental practice?

Many owners open on their own, with an accountant, a fit-out company and a lot of evenings. Help is worth it where a mistake is costly or slow to fix: CQC registration, compliance, hiring and the first months of patient bookings.
