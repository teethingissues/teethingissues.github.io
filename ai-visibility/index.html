What is a ChatGPT visibility audit?

A ChatGPT visibility audit is a check of how easily ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's answer boxes can find, understand and recommend a dental practice. This newer kind of search optimisation is often called GEO, short for generative engine optimisation. Traditional SEO helps a practice appear in Google's list of links. GEO helps a practice appear inside the answer itself when a patient asks ChatGPT for a recommendation. Teething Issues runs two real measurements for every practice. First, a citability score out of 100, which measures how much of the practice website ChatGPT can clearly read and quote. Second, a town-by-town check, which asks ChatGPT for the best private dentists in the practice's town and records whether the practice appears. Most practices have never checked either, and many score around 40 out of 100.

Why does ChatGPT visibility matter for a dental practice?

ChatGPT visibility matters because more patients are starting their search with ChatGPT instead of scrolling through a page of links. When a patient asks ChatGPT for a family dentist, an emergency appointment or Invisalign in a particular town, ChatGPT names a small number of practices and explains why. A practice that is not named in that answer is invisible to that patient, however good the dentistry is. The practices ChatGPT and similar tools recommend are usually the ones whose websites answer patients' questions clearly: fees, finance, emergency appointments, treatments, opening hours and location, written as complete, self-contained answers. The good news is that ChatGPT visibility is still new. Most dental practices, and most of their local competitors, have not started working on it yet, so the practices that fix their websites early have a real head start in their area.

What results can a practice expect?

A practice can expect a measurable improvement, shown with a dated before-and-after, rather than a promise of a perfect score. On the citability measurement, well-run websites typically score between 60 and 75 out of 100, and most dental practices we check start at around 40. No real website scores 100, because the scale rewards writing styles no patient would want to read. What matters most is the town-by-town result: whether the practice now comes up when patients in that town ask ChatGPT for a dentist. For example, we applied exactly the same method to our own newest website, a consumer site for Christmas phone calls, and took the citability score from 32.5 to 62.8 in one afternoon, with no section left graded D or F. Every client gets the same before-and-after, measured with the same tools, so the result is proof rather than marketing.

How can I check if ChatGPT recommends my dental practice?

The quickest way to check is to ask ChatGPT the questions your patients ask, in the way they ask them: for example, “Who is the best private dentist in [your town]?”, “Where can I get Invisalign near [your town]?” or “Which dentist in [your town] takes emergency appointments?”. Note which practices are named, and whether yours is one of them. Try it on a phone and a laptop, because answers can differ, and repeat it every month. Then check your website: does it answer fees, finance, emergencies, treatments, opening hours and location in clear, complete sentences that ChatGPT can quote? Teething Issues does both checks for you, using a citability score out of 100 and a town-by-town record of whether ChatGPT names your practice, then sends you your real score, what’s holding you back and how to fix it, with a dated before-and-after.

What does ChatGPT look for when it recommends a dentist?

When a patient asks ChatGPT to recommend a dentist, it looks for practices it can clearly understand and trust. That usually means a website that answers patients’ real questions in complete, self-contained sentences: what treatments the practice offers, what they cost or start from, whether finance is available, how to get an emergency appointment, opening hours, parking and exactly where the practice is. It also weighs signs of trust from elsewhere, such as Google reviews, a complete Google Business Profile, mentions on other trusted websites and consistent practice details everywhere. Pages that are mostly images, vague slogans or answers hidden in PDFs are hard for ChatGPT to quote, so the practice gets left out. Teething Issues measures how quotable each page is with a citability score out of 100, then rewrites the weakest sections so ChatGPT can name the practice with confidence.

How long does it take for ChatGPT to start recommending my practice?

It usually takes weeks to months, not days. Once the fixes are live on your website, ChatGPT and Google need time to read the updated pages again, and to see the same clear details about your practice elsewhere, such as your Google Business Profile and reviews. Some practices start appearing for a few searches within a few weeks, but it can take up to 6 months for ChatGPT and Google to recommend a practice consistently. That’s why Teething Issues measures before and after with the same tools, re-tests on a set date, and tells you honestly where you stand each time, rather than promising an overnight result. The sooner the fixes go live, the sooner the clock starts, and most local competitors haven’t started yet.

Will my website score 100?

No, and be careful of any company that promises 100 out of 100. The citability scale is a measuring tool, and it rewards a style of writing that no real patient would want to read, so real websites do not score 100. Well-run websites usually score between 60 and 75. What you get from us is more useful: a real, measured improvement, shown with a dated before-and-after using the same tools, and a clear town-by-town record of whether your practice appears when patients ask ChatGPT for a dentist in your area. Most practices start at around 40, so moving into the 60 to 75 range is a big, visible change, and it is the change that affects whether ChatGPT and similar tools recommend you.

Is GEO the same as SEO?

GEO and SEO are related but not the same. SEO, search engine optimisation, helps a website rank in Google's list of links. GEO, generative engine optimisation, helps a website get named inside the answers that ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's overviews write for patients. Many of the fixes help both: clear, complete answers to patients' questions, correct structured data, and a well-organised website. That is why every Teething Issues audit includes an SEO health check alongside the ChatGPT visibility work. A practice can rank well on Google and still be missing from ChatGPT's answers, and the reverse can also happen, which is why we measure both separately.
