What is an ChatGPT visibility audit?

A ChatGPT visibility audit is a check of how easily ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's answer boxes can find, understand and recommend a dental practice. This newer kind of search optimisation is often called GEO, short for generative engine optimisation. Traditional SEO helps a practice appear in Google's list of links. GEO helps a practice appear inside the answer itself when a patient asks ChatGPT for a recommendation. Teething Issues runs two real measurements for every practice. First, an citability score out of 100, which measures how much of the practice website ChatGPT can clearly read and quote. Second, a town-by-town check, which asks ChatGPT for the best private dentists in the practice's town and records whether the practice appears. Most practices have never checked either, and many score around 40 out of 100.

Why does ChatGPT visibility matter for a dental practice?

ChatGPT visibility matters because more patients are starting their search with ChatGPT instead of scrolling through a page of links. When a patient asks ChatGPT for a family dentist, an emergency appointment or Invisalign in a particular town, ChatGPT names a small number of practices and explains why. A practice that is not named in that answer is invisible to that patient, however good the dentistry is. The practices ChatGPT and similar tools recommend are usually the ones whose websites answer patients' questions clearly: fees, finance, emergency appointments, treatments, opening hours and location, written as complete, self-contained answers. The good news is that ChatGPT visibility is still new. Most dental practices, and most of their local competitors, have not started working on it yet, so the practices that fix their websites early have a real head start in their area.

What results can a practice expect?

A practice can expect a measurable improvement, shown with a dated before-and-after, rather than a promise of a perfect score. On the citability measurement, well-run websites typically score between 60 and 75 out of 100, and most dental practices we check start at around 40. No real website scores 100, because the scale rewards writing styles no patient would want to read. What matters most is the town-by-town result: whether the practice now comes up when patients in that town ask ChatGPT for a dentist. Questions practice owners ask

Do you guarantee a score of 100?

No, and any company that guarantees 100 out of 100 should be treated with caution. The citability scale is a measuring tool, and it rewards a style of writing that no real patient would want to read, so real websites do not score 100. Well-run websites usually score between 60 and 75. Teething Issues guarantees something more useful: a real, measured improvement, shown with a dated before-and-after using the same tools, and a clear town-by-town record of whether your practice appears when patients ask ChatGPT for a dentist in your area. Most practices start at around 40, so moving into the 60 to 75 range is a big, visible change, and it is the change that affects whether ChatGPT and similar tools recommend you.

Is GEO the same as SEO?

GEO and SEO are related but not the same. SEO, search engine optimisation, helps a website rank in Google's list of links. GEO, generative engine optimisation, helps a website get named inside the answers that ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's overviews write for patients. Many of the fixes help both: clear, complete answers to patients' questions, correct structured data, and a well-organised website. That is why every Teething Issues audit includes an SEO health check alongside the ChatGPT visibility work. A practice can rank well on Google and still be missing from ChatGPT's answers, and the reverse can also happen, which is why we measure both separately.

The practice simply passes the fixes pack to whoever looks after the website, such as a web developer or the company that built the site, along with our step-by-step instructions.
