How to grow a dental practice: fix the leaks first, then fill the bucket.

For dental entrepreneurs and practice managers. Written by dental care professionals, including a dental care professional, CQC expert and former CQC Registered Manager, who is also a growth business consultant for dental practices.

How do you grow a dental practice?

Grow from the patients you already get before paying for more. Most practices lose patients and income at the same few points, so the fastest growth usually comes from closing those gaps first:

  1. Answer every call, so new patient enquiries become bookings.
  2. Rebook every failed appointment and cancellation, so the diary stays full.
  3. Turn more consultations into treatment, with kind, clear conversations about options, price and finance.
  4. Follow up treatment plans that go cold, instead of waiting for the patient to ring.
  5. Keep patients coming back with recalls, reminders and practice plans.
  6. Then bring in more new patients: Google reviews, being found on Google and ChatGPT, referrals and ads.

Track the numbers for each step every day, so you know where you stand on the 3rd of the month, not the 28th.

Should I spend more on marketing to grow my practice?

Not first. If calls ring out, failed appointments aren't rebooked and consultations don't turn into treatment, more marketing pours more patients into a leaky bucket. Fix the leaks, then spend on marketing, and every new patient is worth more to you.

How do I improve treatment conversion at my practice?

  • Give every consultation a clear structure: listen first, explain options in plain words, then talk about price and finance.
  • Train treatment coordinators, dentists and reception to use the same simple words for price, finance and "I need to think about it".
  • Practise with role-play every week, with real feedback.
  • Follow up every patient who didn't decide on the day, at an agreed time.
  • Keep it kind. Good sales in dentistry means helping patients understand their options and feel confident saying yes to the care they want. Never push.

How can a dental practice get more new patients?

  • Answer every call. A new patient who can't get through rings the next practice on Google.
  • Ask every patient for a Google review. Google's rules say a business must not offer anything in return for a review, and must not ask only the patients it expects to be happy, so ask everyone the same way.
  • Be the practice that answers patients' questions. Clear pages about your treatments, prices and what to expect help you show up when patients search on Google and ask ChatGPT.
  • Make referrals easy for happy patients.
  • Run ads for the treatments you want more of, once the leaks above are fixed.

How do I keep patients coming back?

  • Book the next recall before the patient leaves.
  • Send reminders a few days before and the day before, and make it easy to confirm or rebook.
  • Offer a practice plan that spreads the cost of routine care.
  • Keep a short-notice list, so a cancellation becomes a booking.

Do I need a dental practice consultant to grow?

Not always. Many practices grow by tracking the numbers above and fixing one leak at a time. Outside help is worth it when you don't have the time, your team needs sales training, or you've tried and the numbers haven't moved. Look for people who have worked inside dental practices, who show you the numbers every week, and who tell you the price first.
