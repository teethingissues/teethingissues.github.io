How to recruit a dental nurse: the checks, the paperwork and keeping them.

For practice owners and managers in England. Written by dental care professionals, including a former CQC Registered Manager who has hired and onboarded dental teams.

Is there a dental nurse shortage in the UK?

The number of registered dental nurses is going up, not down. The General Dental Council (GDC) reported 68,472 registered dental nurses at the end of 2025, up 6,473 (5.4%) in a year, and dental nurses are now the largest group on the GDC register. Many practices still find it hard to hire, because good, experienced nurses can choose where they work. So the job is not only finding nurses. It is being the practice they pick, and the practice they stay at.

What checks must a dental practice do before a dental nurse starts?

In England, Regulation 19 of the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014 says you may only employ people who are fit and proper for the role, and Schedule 3 lists the information you must hold for each person. In plain words, for every new dental nurse you need:

  1. Proof of identity, including a recent photograph.
  2. A criminal record certificate (DBS) at the level the role is eligible for.
  3. Satisfactory evidence of conduct in previous health or social care jobs (references).
  4. Where they worked with children or vulnerable adults before, a check of why that job ended, as far as you reasonably can.
  5. Evidence of relevant qualifications.
  6. A full employment history, with a written explanation of any gaps.
  7. Satisfactory information about any physical or mental health conditions relevant to doing the job, after reasonable adjustments.

CQC checks this when it inspects. Missing work history, gaps with no written explanation and references from the wrong person are some of the most common staff file problems.

How do I check a dental nurse's GDC registration?

Search their name or GDC number on the GDC's online register before they start, and save a dated record of the check in their staff file. Qualified dental nurses must be registered with the GDC to work as a dental nurse in the UK. Trainee dental nurses are not on the register yet, so check and record that they are enrolled on a recognised dental nursing course. Re-check registration every year, because registration has to be renewed.

What else goes in a dental nurse's staff file?

Beyond the Schedule 3 list, practices usually keep the signed contract and job description, immunisation records such as hepatitis B (in line with UK Health Security Agency guidance), indemnity arrangements, training certificates such as basic life support and safeguarding, CPD records, and induction sign-off. Each role needs something slightly different, so a receptionist's file and a nurse's file won't look the same.

How can a practice keep good dental nurses?

Nurses stay where they feel trained, trusted and treated fairly. The basics that make the biggest difference are a proper induction so nobody is thrown in, a named person to go to, clear jobs so nobody is guessing, regular one-to-ones and appraisals, CPD support, and a calm practice where problems get fixed instead of ignored. Ask leavers why they are going, and act on what they say.

Sources: Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 19 and Schedule 3 (legislation.gov.uk); General Dental Council, Registration Statistical Report 2025 and GDC news, 7 May 2026; GDC online register; UK Health Security Agency, Immunisation against infectious disease (the Green Book).
