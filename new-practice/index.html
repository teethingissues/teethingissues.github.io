What does a new dental practice need before it opens?

A new dental practice in England needs to be registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC) before it treats patients, which means the provider, the premises and the way the practice will be run are checked first. Alongside registration, a new practice needs its compliance documents in place, a team recruited with complete staff files, and a way to answer and book every patient call from opening day. Teething Issues sets these up together for new and squat practices. The founder is a dental care professional and a former CQC Registered Manager, so CQC registration support, compliance, hiring and phones are handled as one plan rather than four separate jobs. For example, the first team’s DBS checks, Hepatitis B records and GDC registrations are checked before the first patient is booked.

How does Teething Issues help a new practice get its first patients?

Teething Issues builds a pre-opening and first-90-days plan for each new dental practice. The plan covers who the practice is for, what makes it different, and how local people will hear about it before the doors open. From opening day, every call is answered, including at lunch and after five, and every enquiry is followed up, so early interest turns into booked appointments. The owner sees calls, bookings and new patients from week 1, which shows quickly what is working. For example, if calls are coming in but bookings are low, the team can fix the booking conversation in the first month, not the sixth.
