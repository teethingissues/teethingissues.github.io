A former CQC Registered Manager reads the practice’s real documents line by line, gives every gap a named owner and a deadline, and chases it until it is closed. The service covers 14 areas: audits, cleaning, COSHH, CPD, decontamination, fire safety, medical emergencies, risk assessments, staff paperwork, servicing and maintenance, reception, surgery, CQC inspection readiness and the Registered Manager interview. Every staff file is checked against what that role needs, including enhanced DBS, GDC registration, training and CPD, and renewals are flagged well before they run out. The Wisdom Trail turns appraisal gaps into goals, and goals into the right CPD for every team member. Before an inspection, Teething Issues runs a mock inspection and prepares the Registered Manager interview, checking the evidence against CQC’s five key questions: safe, effective, caring, responsive and well-led.

What does CQC look at when it inspects a dental practice?

CQC asks five key questions about every dental practice: is it safe, effective, caring, responsive and well-led. Most dental practices are not given a rating like Good or Outstanding. Instead, the inspection report usually says whether the regulations are met, or whether not all regulations are met. Inspectors look for evidence, not good intentions: up-to-date staff files, training and CPD records, audits, risk assessments, servicing certificates, medical emergency drugs and equipment checks, infection control and decontamination records, and proof that the practice learns from complaints and incidents. They will also usually speak to the Registered Manager about how the practice is run and overseen. Teething Issues checks all of this the way an inspector would, across 14 areas, so the practice knows where it stands long before CQC calls, and every gap has a named owner and a date.

What should we do if our CQC report says not all regulations are met?

Start with the areas the report flagged, which are most often well-led and safe. Well-led usually means governance: audits not done or not acted on, policies out of date, or no clear oversight of who does what. Safe usually means staff checks, infection control, medical emergencies or equipment servicing. Teething Issues turns each point in the report into a clear job with a named owner and a deadline, checks the evidence the way an inspector would, and keeps standards high afterwards so the same gaps don’t come back. The work is led by a former CQC Registered Manager who has led CQC inspections, and the practice team gets simple, clear tasks rather than a pile of paperwork. The owner gets a short update on progress, so they always know what is fixed, what is in hand and what is still to do before CQC returns.

Do we have to do lots of paperwork?

No. Teething Issues does the checking and chasing, so the practice team is never buried in paperwork. Each person just gets clear jobs with a name and a date on each one, such as renewing a certificate, signing off an audit or completing a piece of CPD. Decontamination checks sit on one live checklist that knows what is due and chases what isn’t done. Staff files are checked against what each role needs, and a new starter’s file can be checked in 16 seconds. Renewals for DBS, registrations, training and servicing are flagged early, not the week of an inspection. The owner gets a short update showing what is done, what is in hand and what is next. The aim is a practice that is compliant all year without drowning the team, so everyone knows exactly what is theirs to do and inspections stop being a panic.

Yes.
